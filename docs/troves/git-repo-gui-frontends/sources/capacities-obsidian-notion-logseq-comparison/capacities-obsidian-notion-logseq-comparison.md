# Capacities vs Obsidian vs Notion vs Logseq: 2025 Feature Comparison

By Ann P., Medium, September 1, 2025

## Four Philosophies, One Dilemma

"Your tools are your teachers." The phrase may sound simple, but anyone who has wrestled with thousands of notes understands its weight. Software is not neutral. The systems we adopt do more than hold information. They shape how we think, what we prioritize, and how we connect ideas. A system that feels liberating today can become fragile tomorrow if its underlying philosophy does not align with your way of working.

In 2025, four tools dominate the second brain conversation: Capacities, Obsidian, Notion, and Logseq. At first glance they seem interchangeable, but each carries a distinct conviction about what knowledge is and how it should endure. Capacities promotes schema and structured data. Obsidian grounds its identity in Markdown, where every file belongs to you. Notion thrives on collaboration and design, building for teams first. Logseq embodies openness, using graphs, blocks, and licensing choices to guarantee survival.

The dilemma is not which tool has the better interface. It is which worldview you are ready to inhabit each day.

## Core Convictions — What They Actually Optimize For

Every second brain tool begins with an assumption about how knowledge should be stored and remembered. These assumptions do not always show up on the landing page, but they shape updates, integrations, and even what the platform considers important.

Some systems lean heavily on structure, insisting that order is the only way to achieve resilience. Others invest their faith in durability, trusting plain text to outlast corporate shifts. Still others prioritize collaboration, building environments where teams — not individuals — become the true unit of memory. And finally, a growing camp looks to openness, where transparency and licensing are the ultimate guarantees of survival.

- Structure becomes permanence when schema guides every interaction.
- Collaboration becomes clarity when groups can trust the same source of truth.
- Openness becomes resilience when survival is not tied to a single company.

Together these convictions form a spectrum: Capacities driving schema, Obsidian anchoring permanence, Notion embodying collaboration, and Logseq safeguarding openness. None of them are cosmetic choices. They decide what your second brain will privilege, how it will endure, and ultimately, how it will shape the way you think.

## Shifts That Defined 2024-2025

The years 2024 and 2025 were not just a sequence of updates. They marked a turning point in how each platform revealed its long-term direction. Features are surface signals, but intent is the real message. Obsidian's release of Bases blurred the line between files and databases, showing that structured dashboards could exist entirely inside Markdown. It was not only a technical step forward but a statement that permanence and complexity can coexist without contradiction.

Notion used this period to confront its greatest weaknesses. Offline mode finally stabilized, removing a barrier that had long undermined trust. Calendar stitched time into the workspace, and Q&A transformed search into conversation, allowing teams to query information naturally.

Capacities deepened its schema-first philosophy with automatic exports and linked properties, insisting that portability is not optional but foundational. Logseq reinforced its research focus with stronger whiteboards and PDF annotation while keeping its open-source roots intact. For Logseq, the lesson is simple: survival depends on transparency, not corporate continuity.

## Portability — The Measure of Survival

Portability rarely matters at the beginning of a relationship with software, but it defines the endgame. If a platform pivots, fails, or locks you in, your work risks disappearing with it.

- **Capacities** does not leave it to chance. Automatic scheduled exports produce Markdown, PDF, and CSV copies without user intervention. This turns exit from a desperate emergency into a routine safeguard.
- **Obsidian** answers the question preemptively. By storing every note as a local file from day one, it ensures you never need to "migrate." Its optional Sync service adds encrypted convenience but does not compromise ownership.
- **Notion** pursues portability with an enterprise lens. Structured exports and a robust API make it attractive for organizations that may need to shift data into other platforms. Server residency in the US or EU adds compliance benefits.
- **Logseq** leans on simplicity and openness. Plain text ensures universal readability, while optional Git integration gives version control. Its AGPL license guarantees that if the company folds, forks can keep the ecosystem alive.

## Security and Compliance — The Weight of Trust

Obsidian builds trust through simplicity. Its Sync service is end-to-end encrypted, with no hidden intermediaries. For journalists, writers, and independent creators, this model provides the clarity they need.

Notion approaches trust as a corporate matter. SOC and ISO certifications, HIPAA readiness, and regional data hosting make it suitable for enterprises with regulatory demands.

Logseq relies on transparency and local-first storage. It does not hide its limitations, leaving encryption at rest to the user.

Capacities highlights EU-based hosting and export guarantees. It is less about satisfying large organizations and more about giving individuals and small teams peace of mind.

## AI and Recall — From Noise to Precision

Artificial intelligence has matured, and the standard has shifted. The old question was whether AI could generate. The new question is whether it can recall what matters at the right time.

Notion leads with Q&A. A natural language query now retrieves precise answers across the workspace, including integrations with Slack and Jira. Capacities takes a quieter approach. Its AI autofills properties and drafts summaries, making schema less tedious without overwhelming the experience. Obsidian relies on community creativity. Plugins already allow local model integration, and with Bases, metadata queries are filterable and sortable inside Markdown. Logseq emphasizes context and speed, offering instant block search, annotation tethering, and visual whiteboards.

## Visual Workflows — Beyond the Page

Human thought is not linear. It clusters, circles back, and spreads outward. Visual workflows bring this reality into the design of second brains.

- **Obsidian: infinite canvases for mapping ideas.** Canvas gives users a boundless board where notes, links, and media can be arranged spatially. Writers storyboard narratives, researchers cluster sources, and designers chart processes in ways that linear documents cannot capture.
- **Logseq: whiteboards tied to graph nodes.** Every block can transform into a card that remains linked to the underlying graph. This makes connections not just conceptual but visible, allowing users to see knowledge as a living network rather than isolated entries.
- **Notion: calendar for temporal clarity.** Instead of focusing on spatial arrangement, Notion organizes work around time. Its Calendar integrates tasks and notes with deadlines, ensuring projects stay anchored to schedules.
- **Capacities: structured multi-views.** Tables, galleries, and boards all provide different perspectives on the same underlying object. This schema-driven approach ensures that switching between views never breaks consistency.

## Conclusion — Choosing With Intent

By 2025, Capacities, Obsidian, Notion, and Logseq have matured into more than apps. They are philosophies in software form. Capacities offers schema and safe exits. Obsidian grounds permanence in Markdown files and extends into dashboards. Notion doubles down on collaboration, compliance, and conversational AI. Logseq anchors its identity in openness, graphs, and survival beyond the company.

The real question is not which is "best." It is which aligns with your intent. Do you value structure, permanence, collaboration, or openness?

"A second brain is not storage. It is resilience, recall, and meaning."
