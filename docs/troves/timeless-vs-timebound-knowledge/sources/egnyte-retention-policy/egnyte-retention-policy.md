# Document Retention Policy: Compliance & Best Practices

## The classification model

Enterprise records management classifies documents into retention categories:

1. **Permanent / archival records**: incorporation documents, board minutes, tax returns, IP records, property ownership, litigation settlements, environmental certifications. Never deleted.
2. **Long-term temporary (3–7 years)**: business records, employment records, financial statements, client communications. Deleted after retention period.
3. **Short-term / transitory**: not of long-term value, exist for short-term usage or convenience. Deleted after task completion.

## The lifecycle

Records go through: creation → active use → inactive storage → permanent storage (archival) or destruction.

A record is "closed" when current business activity ends. Closure makes the record **immutable** and begins the mandatory retention period.

## Classification principles

1. **Classify by type and business function**: group documents to apply appropriate retention schedules
2. **Define retention periods per category**: based on legal, regulatory, and operational requirements
3. **Automate retention schedules**: use tools to enforce archival and deletion without human intervention
4. **Maintain audit trails**: track access, edits, and deletions for compliance
5. **Review and update annually**: reassess when regulations or business needs change

## Permanent retention criteria

Documents retained permanently share these properties:
- Ongoing legal obligation
- Historical or research value beyond immediate business use
- Foundational to organizational identity (charters, IP, property)

## From enterprise to personal knowledge management

The enterprise model translates to PKM with modifications:
- **Permanent** → timeless knowledge (math theorems, recipes, physical constants)
- **Long-term temporary** → domain knowledge with known half-lives (engineering practices, medical guidelines)
- **Transitory** → current events, tool versions, framework-specific configuration

The key difference: in PKM, the "business function" is replaced by ongoing relevance to the individual's intellectual life, and the retention schedule is determined by the knowledge half-life of the domain, not regulatory mandates.
