# University Records Retention Schedule — Definitions

## Key classification terms

- **Archival record**: retained permanently for legal, business, historical, or reference purposes. Inactive but preserved.
- **Stored record**: retained for a definite period or permanently. Active enough to warrant organized storage.
- **Transitory record**: not of long-term value. Exists for short-term usage or convenience. Not needed to preserve institutional actions. Generally of temporary value until completion of an action or development of final work product.

## Lifecycle stages

1. Creation/receipt
2. Active use and maintenance
3. Inactive storage
4. Permanent storage (archival) OR destruction

## NARA definitions (federal)

- **Temporary records**: approved for disposal after a specified retention period.
- **Permanent records**: appraised as having sufficient historical or other value to warrant continued preservation by the federal government beyond the time needed for administrative, legal, or fiscal purposes.

## The "transitory" category

This is the category most PKM systems lack. It captures:
- Drafts and working documents superseded by final versions
- Convenience copies retained only for immediate reference
- Information with no lasting administrative, legal, or fiscal value

In rk terms: queries that don't produce durable findings. Episodic records of search activity that don't consolidate into semantic knowledge.

## The closure concept

Records are "closed" when current business activity ends. Closure makes the record **immutable** and begins the retention countdown. This is analogous to a source becoming "stale" in rk — the source is no longer actively being updated, and the TTL clock starts.
