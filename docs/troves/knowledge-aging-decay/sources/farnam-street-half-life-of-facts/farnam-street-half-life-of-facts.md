# The Half-life of Facts

Facts change all the time. Smoking has gone from doctor recommended to deadly. We used to think the Earth was the center of the universe and that Pluto was a planet. Knowledge, like milk, has an expiry date.

## Key findings from Arbesman

Arbesman's core analogy: Knowledge is like radioactivity. Individual facts are unpredictable, but in aggregate they follow measurable decay patterns. A Paris hospital study on cirrhosis and hepatitis literature found a clear decay curve — the half-life of facts in those fields: **45 years**.

From the Economist interview with Arbesman on field-by-field decay rates:

- Mathematics decays slowest: proofs are settled matters
- Medicine has one of the fastest decay rates; it was even faster when transitioning from art to science
- Social sciences decay faster than physical sciences due to experimental noise — effect sizes are smaller with human subjects
- Physical sciences cluster more tightly, making findings more durable

## Key insights

1. Facts, in the aggregate, have half-lives — measurable and predictable
2. Old knowledge isn't waste: "the accumulation of knowledge can then lead us to a fuller and more accurate picture of the world"
3. Asimov's insight: thinking the Earth is spherical is wronger than thinking it's flat — later approximations are progressively less wrong
4. Paradigm shifts: "A prevailing theory or paradigm is not overthrown by the accumulation of contrary evidence, but rather by a new paradigm that, for whatever reasons, begins to be accepted by scientists"
5. Max Planck: "A new scientific truth does not triumph by convincing its opponents and making them see the light, but rather because its opponents eventually die"

## Implications for knowledge management

- Status quo persistence: scientists have decided preferences about beliefs they hold
- Cognitive dissonance creates resistance to updating knowledge
- Medical field leads in combating decay: students taught that everything they learn will be obsolete soon after graduation; "UpToDate" website constantly updates medical textbooks
- Constant effort to explore the world anew is required — even just checking Wikipedia more often
